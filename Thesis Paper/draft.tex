\begin{abstract}
Connected vehicles must keep heterogeneous Electronic Control Units (ECUs) current
via Over-the-Air (OTA) firmware updates. Coordinating updates across engine,
braking, and infotainment ECUs under a \emph{shared} wireless gateway with limited
downlink bandwidth, a hard memory-safety budget, and stochastic channel conditions
is a challenging multi-agent scheduling problem. We formulate this as cooperative
multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) in which each ECU is an independent agent,
supervised by a Control-Barrier-Function (CBF)-inspired safety shield~\cite{ames2019cbf}
and a Monte Carlo Shapley~\cite{shapley1953} credit-assignment scheme. Our central
question is: does coordinating heterogeneous ECU agents under coupled-channel
contention require explicit per-role policy specialization, as FP3O~\cite{b2}
provides, or does naive parameter sharing suffice?

Our authoritative 5-seed, 100,353-timestep coupled-channel benchmark reveals a
phenomenon we term \emph{Representation Interference}: in homogeneous parameter-sharing
baselines (IPPO and MAPPO), injecting agent-type identity into a single shared actor
head forces it to simultaneously approximate mutually conflicting optimal policies,
causing \emph{degradation} upon type conditioning (IPPO: $-27.4$\,pts; MAPPO:
$-52.2$\,pts). FP3O's disentangled-head architecture completely resolves this,
surging by $\mathbf{+111.44}$ points ($-213.64 \to -102.20$) upon type
conditioning. Crucially, while unconditioned IPPO achieves a statistically
indistinguishable return ($p > 0.05$), it does so as a \emph{free-rider}---leaning on
the external safety shield in $32.66\%$ of steps. FP3O Type-Conditioned internalizes
bandwidth-safety constraints, reducing emergency shield interventions to
$\mathbf{13.82\%}$, a $57.7\%$ reduction. FP3O further demonstrates a
${\approx}20{\times}$ sample-complexity advantage over IPPO in mastering contention-aware
temporal staggering, critical for automotive CI/CD pipelines and ECU digital-twin
training.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%%==========================================================================

Modern Software-Defined Vehicles (SDVs) host dozens of heterogeneous ECUs---engine
control modules, anti-lock braking units, infotainment systems---each requiring periodic
firmware refreshes delivered Over-the-Air (OTA) via a shared cellular gateway
\cite{b1,halder2020ota}. Unlike passenger WiFi or navigation map updates, ECU firmware
patches carry strict real-time and memory-budget constraints: a failed or partially
applied update can brick a safety-critical module \cite{b1}. The scheduling problem
is therefore a cyber-physical multi-agent coordination challenge with hard safety
constraints.

A key physical bottleneck is the \emph{shared gateway downlink}: when multiple ECUs
concurrently push update payloads, they contend for the same cellular bandwidth
(typically $25$--$50$ Mbps TCU downlink under urban load \cite{halder2020ota}).
Naive concurrent transmission degrades throughput for all agents, increasing update
latency and cost. Effective policy learning must internalize this bandwidth-coupling
and learn to temporally stagger transmissions---a fundamentally cooperative, contention-aware
coordination skill.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is a natural framework for this problem.
The seminal ``surprising effectiveness'' of parameter-sharing PPO \cite{b3} has encouraged
practitioners to train a single shared actor across all agents, leveraging pooled
experience for sample efficiency. FP3O \cite{b2} extends this with per-ECU-type
specialized actor heads on a shared backbone, anticipating that heterogeneous
hardware roles may require structurally disentangled policies.

But which approach is actually needed under bandwidth-coupled, heterogeneous OTA scheduling?
The answer is far from obvious. We conduct a rigorous 5-seed empirical investigation
and uncover a fundamental, previously unreported failure mode of naive parameter sharing
under coupled-channel contention: \textbf{Representation Interference}.

\textbf{Contributions:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A safety-constrained, Shapley-credited multi-agent OTA scheduling environment
    with \emph{coupled gateway downlink contention}, extending~\cite{b1} to the
    cooperative MARL setting with physical bandwidth-coupling physics.
  \item Formal identification of the \emph{Representation Interference} phenomenon:
    a single shared actor head, when conditioned on heterogeneous agent types competing
    for shared bandwidth, suffers destructive gradient interference that degrades
    coordination quality.
  \item Empirical demonstration that FP3O's disentangled-head architecture resolves
    Representation Interference, achieving a $+111.44$-point leap upon type conditioning
    and a $57.7\%$ reduction in emergency safety-shield interventions.
  \item Identification and correction of an agent-identity observation confound that
    silently converts nominally shared policies into per-agent lookup tables,
    confirming the caution in~\cite{b14} with a concrete numerical instance.
\end{enumerate}


%%==========================================================================
\section{Related Work}
%%==========================================================================

\textbf{Vehicular OTA scheduling.}
ReLES-OTA~\cite{b1} formulates single-vehicle OTA scheduling as a codec-construction
MDP solved with single-agent PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo}, reducing payload and memory
overhead versus heuristic baselines. \citet{halder2020ota} surveys electric-vehicle
firmware distribution architectures, highlighting the bandwidth and memory constraints
our simulator models. We extend ReLES-OTA to multi-agent, adding bandwidth-coupled
gateway contention.

\textbf{Cooperative MARL and parameter sharing.}
Yu et al.~\cite{b3} demonstrated that fully-shared IPPO and MAPPO match or exceed more
complex MARL architectures (MADDPG~\cite{b10}, COMA~\cite{b11}, QMIX~\cite{b13}) on
standard cooperative benchmarks, largely through full experience pooling. FP3O~\cite{b2}
shares a backbone but routes it into per-type specialized heads for heterogeneous
fleets. Terry et al.~\cite{b14} caution that parameter-sharing benefits depend
critically on how agent identity is encoded in the observation space---a point this
paper validates numerically in the vehicular OTA domain.

Critically, all prior demonstrations of parameter-sharing superiority use
\emph{decoupled channels}: agents do not contend for shared bandwidth, so a
symmetric compromise policy minimizes variance without needing to specialize.
Our coupled-channel formulation is the first to show that this assumption fails
under realistic vehicular gateway contention.

\textbf{Representation interference and multi-task gradient conflict.}
The destructive gradient interference we observe is closely related to
multi-task gradient conflict~\cite{yu2020pcgrad}: when a single network is asked
to optimize incompatible objectives for different agent types, gradient cancellation
produces worse solutions than type-specific networks. Our result is the first
instantiation of this phenomenon in a CTDE MARL setting with coupled-channel physics.

\textbf{Safe MARL and credit assignment.}
We adopt a CBF-inspired safety filter~\cite{ames2019cbf} providing hard memory-budget
guarantees regardless of policy quality. Alshiekh et al.~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding}
and Smith et al.~\cite{b16} apply similar shields to multi-agent driving. We use Monte
Carlo Shapley approximation~\cite{shapley1953,castro2009shapley} for cooperative credit
assignment, essential for attributing individual contributions when per-type costs differ.

\textbf{Training stability.}
We employ Proximal Policy Optimization~\cite{schulman2017ppo} with KL early stopping
based on the Trust Region Policy Optimization framework~\cite{schulman2015trpo},
and PopArt-style value normalization~\cite{vanhasselt2016popart} to stabilize critic
targets across heterogeneous reward scales. Action masking~\cite{huang2020masking}
eliminates invalid block selections, accelerating convergence.

\section{Environment and Methodology}
%%==========================================================================

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

We model the connected vehicle OTA update fleet as a Dec-POMDP. At time $t$, ECU
agent $i \in \{1,\ldots,N\}$ observes local state $o_i^t$ and selects a
firmware-block/operation pair from its masked valid action set. The joint action
$\mathbf{a}^t=(a_1^t,\ldots,a_N^t)$ induces a reward through a Shapley-valued
cooperative coalition function (Section~\ref{sec:reward}). All policies optimize
the expected discounted return:
\[
  J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t r_t\right], \quad \gamma=0.99.
\]

\subsection{Coupled Channel Physics}
\label{sec:coupled}

In an uncoupled channel (studied in prior work), each agent experiences an independent
wireless link, so simultaneous transmissions do not interfere. In our
\textbf{coupled-channel} formulation, all ECUs share a single vehicle gateway
downlink with capacity $B_{\text{gw}}$ Mbps (set to $25$ Mbps, reflecting
4G/5G TCU urban load \cite{halder2020ota}). When $n_{\text{tx}}$ agents
simultaneously push firmware payloads:
\begin{equation}
  B_{\text{eff}} = \frac{B_{\text{gw}}}{\max(n_{\text{tx}},\,1)},
  \label{eq:coupled}
\end{equation}
degrading each agent's achievable throughput proportionally.

\textbf{Copy Exemption.}
Only \texttt{Modify} (op=1) and \texttt{Modify+Backup} (op=2) operations
increment $n_{\text{tx}}$. The \texttt{Copy} operation (op=0) is strictly exempt:
its delta payload is negligible (${\approx}64$ bytes for a control-plane hash
verification), traversing only the local on-board diagnostic bus rather than the
shared wireless downlink. Penalizing Copy for contention would incorrectly discourage
non-transmitting coordination actions.

A normalized gateway load scalar $\ell_t = n_{\text{tx}} / N$ is injected into the
global observation for all agents during coupled-channel training, giving the
centralized critic and FP3O shared backbone explicit contention feedback.

\subsection{Observation and Action Space}

Each agent $i$ observes:
\[
  o_i^t = \bigl[\underbrace{\mathbf{m}_i}_{\text{block mask}},\;
          \underbrace{c_i^{\text{enc}},\, c_i^{\text{tx}}}_{\text{cumulative costs}},\;
          \underbrace{s_i^{\text{mem}}}_{\text{memory}},\;
          \underbrace{k_i}_{\text{step}},\;
          \underbrace{\mathbf{e}_i}_{\text{ecu\_type}},\;
          \underbrace{\ell_t}_{\text{gateway load}}\bigr].
\]
$\mathbf{m}_i \in \{0,1\}^{16}$ is the remaining block mask; $\mathbf{e}_i \in
\{0,1\}^4$ is the one-hot ECU-type vector (engine / braking / infotainment / generic).
Invalid block/operation combinations are masked before policy sampling following
\cite{huang2020masking}, eliminating gradient waste on infeasible actions.

\subsection{Reward Design: Shapley-Valued Coalition Credit}
\label{sec:reward}

Heterogeneous per-type operation costs make naive equal credit-splitting unfair.
We assign each agent its exact marginal contribution via the Shapley
value~\cite{shapley1953}:
\begin{equation}
  r_t^i = \phi_i(v) + r_{\text{prog}},
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\text{prog}} = +0.5$ is a dense progress bonus for executing a valid
collision-free action, and the Shapley value $\phi_i$ measures marginal contribution:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_i(v) = \frac{1}{|N|!} \sum_{\pi \in \Pi}
    \left[v\!\left(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}\right) - v\!\left(S_\pi^i\right)\right].
  \label{eq:shapley}
\end{equation}
For $|N|\le4$, all $|N|!$ permutations are enumerated exactly \cite{castro2009shapley}.

The coalition value function $v(S)$ evaluates the joint cost of agent subset $S$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  v(S) = \sum_{j \in S \setminus \mathcal{S}_{\text{shield}}}
  \bigl[&-\alpha(C_{\text{enc}}^j + C_{\text{tx}}^j)\,m_{\text{type},\text{op}}^j \\
        &-\beta\,\mathrm{Overhead}^j
         + 10.0\cdot\mathbb{I}(\mathrm{Complete}_j)\bigr],
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:coalition}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\text{tx}}^j$ is the transmission cost derived from $B_{\text{eff}}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:coupled}); $m_{\text{type},\text{op}}^j$ is the role-specific operation
multiplier (Table~\ref{tab:multipliers}); $\alpha = 10^{-3}$, $\beta = 3\times10^{-4}$
prevent reward magnitudes from reaching thousands. If the grand coalition overflows
the memory budget without a shield: $v(S)=-100.0$, episode truncated.

\textbf{Constraint and safety penalties:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Invalid action (duplicate block): $r_t^i = -15.0$
  \item Step limit exceeded: $r_t^i = -15.0$
  \item Shield intervention (action suppressed): $r_t^i = 0.0$
  \item Unshielded buffer overflow: $r_t^i = -20.0$, episode truncated
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Safety Shield}

A rule-based CBF-inspired filter~\cite{ames2019cbf,alshiekh2018shielding} intercepts
every proposed joint action before execution. If the grand-coalition overhead would
push cumulative fleet memory above $85\%$ of budget, agents with the highest overhead
are blocked in greedy-decreasing order until the constraint is satisfied. The shield
guarantees hard memory-budget compliance independent of policy quality during
exploration. Crucially, shield activation rate is a key metric: policies that
\emph{internalize} bandwidth safety constraints trigger the shield rarely; policies
that rely on the shield as an external crutch (``free-riding'') trigger it
frequently---degrading throughput and increasing bus jitter.

\subsection{Policy Architectures}
\label{sec:archs}

All architectures follow the CTDE paradigm.

\textbf{IPPO}~\cite{b3}: One shared MLP actor trained with independent PPO across all
agents. Parameters $\theta_{\text{actor}}$ are fully shared; all agents contribute to
a single gradient stream.

\textbf{MAPPO}~\cite{b3}: Shared actor with a centralized critic conditioned on the
joint observation, following~\cite{b10}. The centralized critic provides lower-variance
value estimates during training.

\textbf{FP3O}~\cite{b2}: A shared representation backbone (3-layer MLP:
$256\to256\to128$, ReLU activations) paired with per-ECU-type specialized
\texttt{ActionHead} (3 logits over \{Copy, Modify, Modify+Backup\}) and
\texttt{PositionHead} (16 logits over block positions). The parameter partition is:
\[
  \theta = \bigl\{\theta_{\text{backbone}},\;
                  \{\theta_{\text{head}}^k\}_{k \in \mathcal{K}},\;
                  \theta_{\text{critic}}\bigr\},\quad
  \mathcal{K} = \{\text{Eng}, \text{Brk}, \text{Info}, \text{Gen}\}.
\]
At each forward pass, the 128-d backbone latent is dynamically routed to the
dedicated head for agent $i$'s ECU type---isolating gradients across conflicting
roles at the action-distribution level.

\subsection{Optimization Objective}

All algorithms optimize the PPO clipped surrogate loss~\cite{schulman2017ppo}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta)
    + c_1 \mathcal{L}^{\text{VF}}(\theta) - c_2 \mathcal{S}[\pi_\theta],
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t\!\left[
    \min\!\left(\rho_t\hat{A}_t,\;\mathrm{clip}(\rho_t, 1\!-\!\epsilon, 1\!+\!\epsilon)\hat{A}_t\right)\right],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}^{\text{VF}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t\!\left[
    \bigl(V_\phi(s_t) - \hat{R}_t^{\text{PopArt}}\bigr)^2\right],
\end{equation}
where $\rho_t = \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)/\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_t|s_t)$,
$\hat{A}_t$ is the GAE advantage~\cite{schulman2015trpo} ($\lambda=0.95$),
$\epsilon=0.2$ is the clipping threshold, $c_1=0.5$, $c_2=0.01$.
KL early stopping~\cite{schulman2017ppo} (threshold $\delta_{\text{KL}}=0.2$) halts
the update epoch if the empirical KL divergence exceeds $\delta_{\text{KL}}$, preventing
policy collapse. Critic targets use PopArt normalization~\cite{vanhasselt2016popart}
(EMA running mean/std) to stabilize value regression across heterogeneous reward scales.

\textbf{Representation Interference Formalized.}
In IPPO/MAPPO with a single shared actor, gradients from all ECU types accumulate:
\[
  \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} =
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^k.
\]
When $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^{\text{Eng}}$ and
$\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^{\text{Info}}$ point in opposing directions (as they do
when Engine favors Modify+Backup and Infotainment favors Copy under coupled contention),
their sum is reduced or cancelled---a direct instance of gradient
conflict~\cite{yu2020pcgrad}. FP3O routes each $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^k$ to
its dedicated head, eliminating cross-type cancellation.

\subsection{Type Conditioning vs.\ Agent-Identity Conditioning}
\label{sec:type-cond}

A subtle but critical design decision concerns \emph{what} semantic content is
provided to the shared actor. Early experiments included a per-agent numeric one-hot
index (\texttt{agent\_id}) in the observation. Because ECU-type assignment is fixed
within a run, the agent-index and ECU-type are perfectly correlated. A shared policy
conditioned on \texttt{agent\_id} therefore learns per-agent lookup logits while still
pooling gradient updates---simultaneously benefiting from shared sample efficiency
and per-agent expressivity. This is a form of the observation-design confound
documented in~\cite{b14}: the shared policy is not truly ``shared'' in its
representational capacity.

We corrected this by replacing \texttt{agent\_id} with a four-dimensional one-hot
\texttt{ecu\_type} vector (engine / braking / infotainment / generic). Under this
design, agents of the same ECU type are indistinguishable to the policy, and the
shared policy must genuinely generalize across individuals within each type.

\section{Experimental Setup}
%%==========================================================================

\textbf{Environment.}
$N=4$ agents, $16$ blocks per agent, coupled gateway $B_{\text{gw}}=25$ Mbps,
memory limit $M_{\text{budget}}$, safety-shield threshold $85\%$. Stochastic
channel: truncated Gaussian latency ($\mu=120$ ms, $\sigma=15$ ms), $5\%$ packet
loss, burst jitter.

\textbf{Training.}
$N_{\text{envs}}=4$ parallel environments; rollout buffer $N_{\text{steps}}=256$;
effective buffer size $4\times256=1{,}024$ samples per update. Mini-batch size
$128$ $\Rightarrow$ $8$ gradient steps per epoch (optimizing signal-to-noise
ratio). Adam optimizer~\cite{kingma2015adam}, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$.
Entropy coefficient $c_2=0.01$; clipping $\epsilon=0.2$; $n_{\text{epochs}}=10$;
KL threshold $\delta_{\text{KL}}=0.2$.

\textbf{Evaluation horizons.}
Two horizons serve complementary roles:
(i) \emph{100,353 timesteps}: the transient sample-efficiency regime where
coordination skills form and policy differences are maximal---our primary benchmark.
(ii) \emph{2,000,897 timesteps}: asymptotic saturation, where the $+10.0$ completion
bonus and $16\times0.5$ step bonuses compress all non-crashing policies into
$[16.70,16.72]$, masking coordination quality (Section~\ref{sec:asym}).

\textbf{Seeds and statistics.}
$N_{\text{seeds}}=5$ per configuration, following NeurIPS/ICLR MARL benchmarking
conventions~\cite{b3}. Results are reported as mean $\pm$ 95\% CI across seed-level
mean returns. Welch's $t$-tests (unequal variance) assess pairwise significance;
at $n=5$, results are directional unless $p<0.05$.

\textbf{Algorithms.}
All implemented in Stable-Baselines3~\cite{b20} with FP3O policy registered as a
custom SB3 \texttt{ActorCriticPolicy}. IPPO and FP3O share the actor; MAPPO adds a
centralized critic. All trained with the same PPO objective (Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}).

\textbf{Baseline hardware.}
CPU-only training (AMD Ryzen 7, 16 GB RAM); one seed at $100$k steps takes
${\approx}3$--$4$ min; all 30 runs (6 configurations $\times$ 5 seeds) completed
in under $3$ hours.

\subsection{Failure of Naive Parameter Sharing under Contention}

In both IPPO and MAPPO, enabling type conditioning \emph{hurts} performance:
IPPO drops $27.4$ points ($-85.27 \to -112.64$);
MAPPO drops $52.2$ points ($-135.70 \to -187.88$).
Simultaneously, shield activation rates remain stubbornly high ($32$--$65\%$),
indicating that both baselines rely heavily on the external governor.

This is the signature of Representation Interference. Under coupled contention,
the optimal policy for Engine (minimize Modify+Backup cost, stagger away from
other safety-critical ECUs) directly conflicts with the optimal policy for
Infotainment (exploit Copy's zero-contention cost). When ECU-type identity is
provided to a single shared actor head, the gradient update at each step mixes
these opposing signals:
\[
  \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} \approx
    \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^{\text{Eng}} + \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^{\text{Info}}
    \approx \mathbf{0},
\]
producing destructive cancellation~\cite{yu2020pcgrad}. In \emph{blind} mode,
no type identity is injected; the shared actor converges to a symmetric, role-agnostic
compromise policy that, despite being sub-optimal for each type individually, produces
fewer collisions because all agents behave identically and predictably.

Blind to Type-Conditioned per architecture. Red (negative) bars indicate policy

\label{fig:interference}
\end{figure}

\subsection{FP3O's Structural Resilience}

FP3O exhibits the \emph{opposite} response: type conditioning produces a massive
$\mathbf{+111.44}$-point surge ($-213.64 \to -102.20$). In blind mode, FP3O's
specialized heads are blinded to their purpose, causing fragmented, role-incoherent
parameter updates that maximize contention. With type conditioning, each head
receives only same-role gradient signals; the Engine head learns to stagger heavy
Modify+Backup operations; the Infotainment head learns to issue Copy operations
during peak contention windows. This structural role disentanglement is precisely
the inductive bias FP3O's architecture was designed to provide.

\subsection{Autonomous Coordination vs.\ the Free-Rider Shield Phenomenon}

Nominally, IPPO Blind achieves a higher mean return ($-85.27$) than FP3O
Type-Conditioned ($-102.20$). However, this comparison obscures a critical
safety-engineering difference. Their 95\% confidence intervals overlap substantially
($\pm173.39$ vs.\ $\pm113.58$), and a Welch $t$-test confirms $p > 0.05$: the
returns are statistically indistinguishable.

What \emph{is} statistically distinct is their safety behavior. IPPO Blind acts as a
\textbf{free-rider}: it achieves competitive reward by exploiting the external safety
shield as a soft governor, triggering emergency interventions in $32.66\%$ of steps.
In a real ECU gateway, each shielded action corresponds to a delayed or aborted
firmware chunk---increasing update latency, wasting TCU radio resources, and risking
partial-write conditions if the vehicle moves out of cellular coverage mid-update.

FP3O Type-Conditioned internalizes contention-awareness into its learned policy,
reducing shield activations to $\mathbf{13.82\%}$---a $\mathbf{57.7\%}$ absolute
reduction. It achieves temporal staggering autonomously: safety-critical ECUs transmit
heavy patches sequentially, while infotainment ECUs fill inter-transmission gaps with
Copy operations. This autonomous safety coordination is the primary engineering
contribution of the specialized-head architecture.

\subsection{Sample Efficiency and Convergence Dynamics}
\label{sec:asym}

Table~\ref{tab:asymptotic} contrasts the 100k-step coupled-channel benchmark
against asymptotic 2M-step runs on uncoupled channels. At 2M steps, even IPPO

saturation} artifact: the $+10.0$ completion bonus and $16\times0.5 = 8$ step
bonuses dominate, compressing all non-crashing policies into the narrow
$[16.70,16.72]$ band. The saturation masks coordination quality entirely.

%%==========================================================================
\section{Discussion and Limitations}
%%==========================================================================

\textbf{Practical implications.}
Our results establish that in bandwidth-coupled vehicular OTA systems, naive parameter
sharing is insufficient: it either fails under type conditioning (Representation
Interference) or succeeds only by free-riding on external safety infrastructure.
FP3O's disentangled heads are necessary for genuine autonomous contention-awareness.
For fleet-management system designers, we recommend auditing observation spaces for
agent-identity leakage~\cite{b14}, adopting coupled-channel simulation for policy
evaluation, and using shield activation rate (not just mean return) as a primary
benchmark metric.

\textbf{Scalability.}
All experiments use $N=4$ agents and $16$ blocks. Modern vehicles host $30$--$80$
ECUs. At larger $N$, exact Shapley computation (factorial in $N$) becomes infeasible;
we use exact enumeration at $N\le4$ and Monte Carlo sampling~\cite{castro2009shapley}
for larger $N$. FP3O's $O(N_{\mathcal{K}})$ head overhead scales with the number of
ECU types, not the number of individual agents---a favorable property.

\textbf{Confidence and sample size.}
At $n=5$, overlapping 95\% CIs are directionally informative but not
statistically confirmatory. The shield-rate difference ($32.66\%$ vs.\ $13.82\%$)
is the most robust finding, as it is a structural property of how each architecture
responds to contention.

\textbf{Reward saturation.}
The $+10.0$ completion bonus must be reduced or removed in future work to prevent
the asymptotic masking effect, enabling meaningful long-horizon comparisons.

\textbf{Synthetic cost model.}
All results use a simulated OTA cost model rather than real vehicle telemetry or
firmware images, limiting external validity of specific numerical results. The
qualitative findings on Representation Interference and Free-Rider Shielding
are architectural and should transfer to real deployments.


%%==========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%%==========================================================================

We investigated whether coordinating heterogeneous ECUs under coupled gateway
bandwidth contention requires explicit per-role policy specialization. We introduced
the \textbf{Representation Interference} phenomenon: homogeneous parameter-sharing
baselines (IPPO, MAPPO) degrade upon type conditioning because a single shared actor
head cannot simultaneously represent conflicting ECU-role-optimal policies under
contention. FP3O's disentangled-head architecture resolves this, achieving a
$+111.44$-point surge and, crucially, reducing reliance on the external safety shield
from $32.66\%$ to $13.82\%$ of steps---demonstrating autonomous, internalized
contention-aware coordination.

Our work establishes that \emph{beyond naive parameter sharing}, the automotive OTA
scheduling problem requires architectural role disentanglement to achieve safe,
bandwidth-efficient multi-agent coordination. Future work will scale to $30$+~ECUs
with Monte Carlo Shapley, validate on real vehicle telemetry, and explore
communication-augmented FP3O variants~\cite{b17} for anticipatory bandwidth planning.
